# Equality

## Definition

Equality means that independent human beings are treated equally before the law.

Equality does not mean that all people are identical.

Human beings differ in talents, abilities, experiences, wealth, and circumstances.

Equality means that these differences must not become a justification for denying human dignity.

---

## Core Principle

Independent persons must be treated as equals before the law.

---

## Historical Perspective

Throughout history, many societies were organized according to status, rank, class, race, lineage, or privilege.

Feudal societies assigned rights and obligations according to social position.

Modern society emerged by rejecting these structures and recognizing all persons as legal equals.

This principle became one of the foundations of constitutional government and civil society.

---

## Numa Theory Perspective

Equality is the second condition of civil society.

Human dignity alone is insufficient.

Independence alone is insufficient.

When independent persons recognize one another as equals, the possibility of freedom emerges.

Therefore:

Independence → Equality → Freedom

The order is essential.

Without independence, equality becomes dependency.

Without equality, freedom becomes domination.

---

## Legal Perspective

Equality before the law is a fundamental principle of modern legal systems.

No person should receive privileges or disadvantages solely because of:

* birth
* status
* race
* ethnicity
* religion
* gender
* social class

The law must recognize all persons as equal legal subjects.

---

## AI Era Perspective

Artificial Intelligence may unintentionally reproduce inequality through biased data, algorithms, or concentrated technological power.

Therefore equality requires continuous observation and correction.

Technology must not create new forms of digital hierarchy.

AI should assist the realization of equality, not undermine it.
