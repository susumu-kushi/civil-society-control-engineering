# Numa Theory: The Person in Legal Science
## Rights, Capacity, and the Legal Construction of the Individual

## Core Principle

> Human beings must never be treated merely as objects.

## 1. From "Human Being" to "Person" — The Legal Transformation

The previous page (Page 03) established the fundamental distinction between the biological "human being" and the legal "person."

This page examines how that distinction operates within legal science itself — how law takes the concrete, unequal human being and constructs the abstract, equal legal person through specific legal techniques.

The key insight of Numa Theory is this:

> Modern law does not simply declare that all human beings are equal.
> It constructs equality through a precise set of legal instruments.

---

## 2. The Standard Person — The Atomic Individual

The foundation of modern private law is the concept of the **atomic individual**: a standard model of the legal person, abstracted from all natural and social particularity.

This standard person:
- Possesses their own independent will
- Expresses that will in their own words
- Enters into agreements with others on equal terms
- Takes responsibility for their own legal acts
- Pursues their own life plan under their own authority

Modern law assumes this model as its baseline.
Every rule of contract, property, and obligation is built upon it.

The atomic individual is permitted — indeed, expected — to engage in the competition of social life on their own terms.
This is the expression of **freedom** in law.

---

## 3. Legal Capacity — The Universal Foundation

The first and most fundamental legal instrument through which the atomic individual is constructed is **legal capacity (権利能力)**.

Legal capacity is the capacity to hold rights and bear duties.

Under modern civil law, **every person possesses legal capacity from the moment of birth**.
It cannot be taken away. It does not depend on wealth, status, intelligence, or social position.

This universality is the legal expression of **equality**.

It means that every human being — regardless of their concrete differences — stands in the same position before the law as a bearer of rights.

In historical perspective, this was a revolutionary achievement.
In feudal society, legal standing depended on status and rank.
Not everyone could hold property, enter contracts, or bring claims before a court.

The modern principle — that legal capacity belongs to every person as a matter of right — was the legal foundation of the transition from feudal society to civil society.

---

## 4. Capacity to Act — The Instrument of Autonomy

Legal capacity alone is not sufficient.
To participate actively in legal life — to make contracts, create obligations, transfer property — a person must also possess **capacity to act (行為能力)**.

Capacity to act is the capacity to perform valid legal acts through one's own will.

Modern law presumes that every adult person of sound mind possesses full capacity to act.
This presumption is the legal expression of **independence** — the recognition that every person is capable of directing their own legal life.

However, capacity to act is not absolute.
For those who cannot exercise it — infants, young children, persons with severe cognitive disabilities — law provides a supplementary mechanism: the **legal representative**.

The legal representative acts in place of the person, ensuring that their legal interests are protected even when they cannot act for themselves.

This is not a denial of the person's legal status.
It is the law's way of **bridging the gap** between the ideal of the atomic individual and the reality of human vulnerability.

---

## 5. Two Categories — and the Law's Response

Numa Theory identifies a fundamental division within modern law's treatment of persons:

### Category A: The Standard Person (Atomic Individual)
Possesses both legal capacity and full capacity to act.
Can participate in the competition of social life on equal terms.
The primary subject of private law — contract, property, obligation.

### Category B: Those Who Cannot Meet the Standard
Possess legal capacity but lack full capacity to act.
Cannot participate in legal life without assistance.

For Category B, modern law applies what Numa Theory calls **legal processing**:

| Situation | Legal Response |
|---|---|
| Infant / young child | Legal representative (parent or guardian) |
| Person with cognitive disability | Guardian appointed by court |
| Person without property or resources | Maintenance obligations, social welfare, redistribution |

The goal of legal processing is always the same: to bring every human being **as close as possible** to the standard of the atomic individual — to make independence, equality, and freedom real, not merely formal.

## 6. The Two Instincts in Legal Form

Numa Theory traces this structure back to two fundamental instincts that modern law incorporates from social life:

### The Instinct of Competition
Every person pursues their own interests as a free and equal individual.
Law enables this through contract freedom, property rights, and market mechanisms.
This is **law as technology** — the institutional framework for social competition.

### The Instinct of Mutual Assistance
When competition threatens to destroy those who cannot stand alone, society intervenes to protect them.
Law expresses this through family law, labor law, social security, and consumer protection.
This is **law as ethics** — the institutional framework for social solidarity.

Modern law holds these two instincts in tension.
Neither can be abandoned.
The task of legal science is to find the right balance — always measured against the ideal of civil society:

> **The independence, equality, and freedom of every person.**

## 7. Natural Persons and Legal Persons — The Two Inhabitants of Legal Space

Legal science recognizes two kinds of legal person, both of whom inhabit the space of rights and duties:

### Natural Persons
Individual human beings.
Legal capacity attaches to every natural person from birth.
The natural person is the primary subject of modern law — the one whose dignity, independence, and freedom law exists to protect.

### Legal Persons (Juridical Persons)
Collective entities — corporations, municipalities, associations, foundations — to which law grants legal personality.
Legal persons can hold property, enter contracts, and bear obligations.
They are constructed by law to serve human purposes — and remain accountable to the ideals of civil society.

Numa Theory's perspective on legal persons is precise:
A legal person is a **legal technique**, not a natural entity.
It exists to serve the interests of the natural persons who constitute it.
When a legal person acts against the dignity and freedom of natural persons, it has exceeded its legitimate purpose.

## 8. The Person in Legal Science — A Summary

The concept of the "person" in legal science is the technical foundation of civil society.

It is the instrument through which:
- Every human being is recognized as a bearer of rights
- Equality is constructed across natural and social difference
- Independence is institutionalized as a legal presumption
- Freedom is given concrete legal form

But it is always also a **standard to be achieved**, not merely a status to be declared.

The gap between the legal "person" and the living "human being" is the permanent challenge of modern law.
Closing that gap — through legal processing, through welfare institutions, through the ongoing reform of law — is the permanent task of legal science.

As Numa Theory states:

> Modern law introduces and manages the two instincts of competition and mutual assistance
> in order to achieve its great ideal:
> **the independence, equality, and freedom of every person who constitutes modern society.**
