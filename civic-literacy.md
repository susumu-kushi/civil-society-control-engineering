Civic Literacy

What Is Civic Literacy?

Civic Literacy is the ability to understand oneself as an independent citizen rather than as a subject of power.

It is the ability to understand the principles, institutions, and responsibilities that support a civil society.

In modern society, citizens are not objects of the state.

Citizens are the sovereign members of society.

Civic Literacy helps individuals understand this position and exercise it responsibly.

Why Civic Literacy Matters

Without Civic Literacy:

* Citizens become dependent on authority.
* Citizens may surrender their freedom voluntarily.
* Citizens may accept injustice without recognizing it.
* Citizens may become objects of political, economic, or technological control.

Civic Literacy enables citizens to recognize these risks and respond as independent participants in society.

Civic Literacy and Democracy

Democracy cannot function through elections alone.

Citizens must understand:

* Why governments exist.
* Why laws exist.
* Why rights exist.
* Why responsibilities exist.

Civic Literacy allows citizens to evaluate governments, corporations, and institutions according to the principles of civil society.

Civic Literacy in the AI Age

In the past, Civic Literacy focused primarily on law, government, and social institutions.

In the AI age, Civic Literacy must also include understanding digital systems.

Algorithms increasingly influence:

* Information
* Commerce
* Education
* Employment
* Political communication

Citizens must therefore understand both civic principles and digital systems.

For this reason:

Civic Literacy and IT Literacy can no longer be separated.

Together they help citizens remain independent in the age of AI.
