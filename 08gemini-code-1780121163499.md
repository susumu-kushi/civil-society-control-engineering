## 1. The Relationship Between "Goodness" and "Justice"

[cite_start]Numa Theory begins by drawing a sharp distinction between two primary ideals: **Goodness (善)** and **Justice (正義)**[cite: 1, 2, 3].

### Goodness
[cite_start]The highest ideal of morality[cite: 4]. [cite_start]It represents a broad, comprehensive virtue aimed at personal character, the completion of human personality, and the realization of a virtuous life[cite: 4].

### Justice
[cite_start]The ideal of law[cite: 6]. [cite_start]It is that specific part of moral virtue that concerns itself directly with the **normative order of social life**[cite: 5].

[cite_start]Therefore, justice is not a vague, abstract moral sentiment[cite: 7]. [cite_start]It serves as the definitive standard—the "ideal of law"—used to evaluate, criticize, and guide concrete, existing legal institutions[cite: 6]. [cite_start]It dictates how the rules of social life ought to be constructed[cite: 7].

## 2. Universal Justice and Particular Justice

[cite_start]To understand how justice operates, Numa Theory traces the classical distinction inherited since Aristotle between two levels of justice[cite: 8, 9]:

### Universal (Comprehensive) Justice — 一般的正義
[cite_start]The overarching moral virtue that runs through social life as a whole[cite: 10, 11]. [cite_start]It defines "how a human being ought to be," encompassing not just adherence to legal rules, but also consideration for others, a sense of social responsibility, and the broader qualities of a "good citizen"[cite: 11, 12].

### Particular (Partial) Justice — 特殊的正義
[cite_start]The fairness of distributions, transactions, and exchanges within limited domains of society[cite: 13, 14]. [cite_start]This level of justice directly connects to concrete legal fields and is traditionally subdivided into two categories[cite: 15, 16]:

* [cite_start]**Distributive Justice (配分の正義 / 配分的正義):** How public goods, resources, offices, and burdens are allocated among individuals according to their merits, needs, or capacities (e.g., tax law, social security)[cite: 15, 17, 40].
* [cite_start]**Commutative/Corrective Justice (交換的正義):** Ensuring that equivalence and equilibrium are maintained in contracts, transactions, and rectifications (e.g., contract law, civil liability)[cite: 15, 18, 40].

## 3. The Tension Between Justice and Concrete Law: The Prohibition of Killing

[cite_start]To illustrate the critical tension between the ideal of justice and the reality of enacted law, Numa Theory examines the foundational criminal norm: **"You must not kill another human being."** [cite: 20, 21]

[cite_start]Intuitively, this appears to be an absolute rule of justice heavily laden with moral imperatives[cite: 22]. [cite_start]However, an examination of the actual legal system reveals necessary exceptions[cite: 23, 26]:
- [cite_start]Self-defense (正当防衛) [cite: 24]
- [cite_start]Necessity (緊急避難) [cite: 25]
- [cite_start]State-sanctioned killings, such as lawful warfare or the execution of capital punishment [cite: 26]

[cite_start]This reality reveals a crucial pragmatic point: **Law cannot be designed by abstract ideals of justice alone.** [cite: 28] [cite_start]It must inevitably adapt to the actual structures, conflicting interests, and security constraints of the living society[cite: 28].

[cite_start]Conversely, if a state expands these exceptions purely based on political convenience or realpolitik, the law rapidly degenerates into a mere tool of arbitrary power[cite: 30]. [cite_start]Therefore, civil society requires an independent ideal of justice capable of criticizing the law from within[cite: 31].

## 4. Procedural Justice and Substantive Justice

[cite_start]When evaluating whether a legal order is genuinely just, Numa Theory identifies two distinct dimensions that must be examined[cite: 32, 33]:

### Procedural Justice (手続の正義)
[cite_start]The requirement that individuals in identical circumstances must be treated identically (formal equality)[cite: 34, 35]. [cite_start]It demands that judicial and administrative procedures remain public, predictable, consistent, and free from arbitrary whim[cite: 36]. [cite_start]It focuses entirely on **how legal norms are applied**[cite: 37].

### Substantive Justice (内容の正義)
[cite_start]The requirement that the content of the legal norm itself must be fair and reasonable in how it distributes societal benefits and burdens[cite: 38, 39]. [cite_start]Fields like tax law, labor law, and social security must be rigorously examined through the lens of distributive and commutative justice to determine who bears the burden and who receives protection[cite: 40].

[cite_start]A law that imposes a grossly unfair burden or systematically tramples upon the dignity of specific groups fails the test of substantive justice[cite: 41, 42, 43]. [cite_start]Such a norm must be exposed, criticized, and targeted for reform or abolition[cite: 44].

## 5. The Dual Role of Justice

[cite_start]Numa Theory cautions against treating justice purely as an absolute, utopian ideal[cite: 45, 46]. [cite_start]Divorced from the practical design and operation of actual legal systems, justice risks becoming a powerless, empty theory[cite: 46]. 

[cite_start]Instead, justice must be understood as fulfilling a **dual role**[cite: 45, 47]:

### Justice as a Critical Principle (批判原理としての正義)
[cite_start]A relentless yardstick used to cross-examine existing legal institutions and ask: *"Is this truly fair?"* [cite: 48, 49] [cite_start]It serves as the primary theoretical weapon to expose unjust discrimination, oppression, and abuses of state power[cite: 50].

### Justice as a Practical Standard (現実的尺度としての正義)
[cite_start]A benchmark that measures what can realistically be achieved under current social conditions[cite: 51, 52]. [cite_start]While acknowledging the necessity of compromise, it provides a compass to adjust and refine the law in a direction that fosters social cohesion and civil development[cite: 53].

[cite_start]Justice is not a "sword of judgment" swung from completely outside the legal order; it is the conceptual axis utilized *within* civil society to constantly reshape and reconstruct the law[cite: 54].

## 6. The True Nature of Legal Science: Bridging Sein and Sollen

[cite_start]Custom, morality, and law all belong to the world of *Sollen*—the normative realm of "what ought to be." [cite: 57] [cite_start]Within this landscape, modern law is a unique system: it handles individuals as independent, equal, and free legal persons, backed by the organized compulsive power of the state to sustain and develop civil society[cite: 58, 59, 60, 61]. 

[cite_start]Consequently, Numa Theory redefines the very definition of legal science, stripping away the narrow misconceptions that plague traditional academia[cite: 126, 128]:

> [cite_start]**Legal science is not the mere interpretation of statutory clauses or the analytical cataloging of judicial precedents.** [cite: 126]

[cite_start]Rather, it is a dual-structured discipline[cite: 73]:

* [cite_start]**Analyzing reality as it is (*Sein*):** Thoroughly observing and diagnosing the actual distributions of power, wealth, historical contexts, literature, and social structures[cite: 73, 75, 80].
* [cite_start]**Realizing the ideal (*Sollen*):** Designing and verifying legal norms that actively construct the modern civil society where every individual can stand as an independent, equal, and free subject[cite: 76, 131].

### Why Must Legal Science Analyze the Reality of *Sein*?
[cite_start]Legal science must rigorously analyze and evaluate empirical reality because **the standard model of the human person—the bearer of rights and duties—cannot be extracted from abstract philosophy alone.** [cite: 146, 149] [cite_start]It must be drawn from the living, evolving human world[cite: 149].

[cite_start]Concepts like legal capacity, capacity to act, "the reasonable person," and self-determination are empty air if they ignore actual social conditions[cite: 101, 102, 108, 154]. [cite_start]Because human beings, their modes of living, and their technological environments continuously evolve, the "standard person" assumed by modern law must be dynamically updated[cite: 98, 112, 119]. 

[cite_start]If legal scholars abandon this critical diagnostic function and lock themselves inside mere text-interpretation, the law detaches from reality[cite: 83, 123]. [cite_start]It fails its noble purpose to serve as the structural bridge connecting *Sein* and *Sollen*[cite: 123, 135].

[cite_start]This is precisely why Numa Theory demands that scholars engage with art, philosophy, evolutionary science, and literature[cite: 69, 78, 80]. [cite_start]To build a law that protects human dignity, one must first understand the multidimensional depth of the living human experience[cite: 80, 81].
