# Numa Theory: The Definition of Law

## Introduction

After examining law in relation to custom, morality, religion, and justice, an important question remains:

What is law?

Numa Theory approaches this question cautiously.

The purpose is not to discover an absolute definition valid for all times and places.

Rather, it is to establish a practical definition that allows legal science to analyze and evaluate social reality.

---

## Why Define Law?

Law exists within a broader world of social norms.

Human society is governed by many forms of normative guidance, including:

- Custom
- Morality
- Religion
- Law

All of these belong to the world of Sollen.

All attempt to answer the question:

> How ought human beings to live?

However, legal science requires a more precise concept.

Without a working definition, it becomes impossible to determine what belongs within the field of legal analysis and what does not. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

---

## Definition Through Genus and Differentia

Classical logic teaches that a concept should be defined through:

- A broader category (genus)
- A distinguishing characteristic (differentia)

Law is therefore first understood as a type of social norm.

The next task is to identify the characteristic that distinguishes law from other forms of social regulation. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

---

## Conventional Definitions and Their Limits

Many traditional definitions describe law as:

- A command of the state
- A rule backed by coercion
- A norm regulating external conduct through organized force

These definitions capture part of the truth.

Law is connected with state power and organized coercion.

However, such definitions are incomplete.

They reduce law to command and force while overlooking its relationship to social order, justice, and civil society. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

## Law as a Social Norm

Numa Theory begins from a broader perspective.

Law belongs to the family of social norms.

Like morality and custom, law seeks to guide human behavior.

Its purpose is not merely to punish wrongdoing.

Its deeper purpose is to maintain social cooperation and enable human coexistence.

Law therefore emerges from the realities of social life rather than from state power alone. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

---

## The Distinguishing Characteristic of Law

What distinguishes law from other social norms?

The answer is organized state support.

Law is a system of social norms that is:

- Recognized by the state
- Applied by the state
- Supported by organized coercive institutions

Courts, administrative agencies, and legal sanctions provide law with a level of institutional force that morality, religion, and custom do not possess.

This organized support allows law to function as a stable framework for social life. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

## Law, Order, and Justice

Law cannot be reduced to coercion.

A legal system that relies solely on force becomes indistinguishable from domination.

Law must also be evaluated according to justice.

The legal order exists to maintain social cooperation, but it must also remain open to criticism from the standpoint of justice.

In this sense:

- Order without justice becomes oppression.
- Justice without legal order becomes instability.

Law exists between these two dangers. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

---

## Law, Sein, and Sollen

Numa Theory understands legal science as a bridge between Sein and Sollen.

Legal science must:

1. Observe and analyze existing social reality.
2. Identify the human beings who serve as subjects of rights and duties.
3. Construct legal norms capable of realizing civil society.

The purpose of legal analysis is therefore not merely to interpret statutes.

It is to understand society and to guide legal development toward independence, equality, and freedom. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

---

## Working Definition of Law

From the perspective of Numa Theory, law may be defined as:

> A system of social norms, supported by the organized coercive power of the state, which directs human conduct and maintains social order while remaining subject to evaluation through the ideal of justice.

This definition emphasizes:

- Social norms
- State support
- Social order
- Justice

All four elements are necessary for understanding law.

---

## Conclusion

Law is not merely a command.

Nor is it merely a moral ideal.

Law is a system of social norms that operates within society through organized institutions and state support.

Its purpose is to maintain social order while contributing to the realization of civil society.

Legal science therefore studies both reality and ideals.

It analyzes Sein and seeks to realize Sollen.
