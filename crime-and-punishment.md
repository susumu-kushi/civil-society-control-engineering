# Crime and Punishment

## Author

Susumu Kushi

---

## Summary

This book examines one of the most fundamental questions of political and legal philosophy:

Why does the state possess the power to punish?

Modern society grants the state a monopoly on legitimate coercion.

The state may arrest, imprison, fine, and in some jurisdictions even execute individuals.

Why is such power permitted?

Where is the boundary between the individual and the state?

This book explores that boundary through the perspectives of law, justice, civil society, and Numa Theory.

---

## Core Question

What justifies the state's power to punish?

---

## Core Principle

Law is a system that legitimizes coercion.

The central challenge is determining when coercion is justified and when it becomes domination.

## Central Message

A modern state exists to protect human beings.

When punishment ceases to protect human dignity and becomes a tool of domination, the state loses its legitimacy.

The question is not whether coercion exists.

The question is whether coercion serves human dignity.
