# Numa Theory: The Classification of Society
## How Human Society Is Structured — and Why It Matters for Law

---

## Core Principle

> Human beings must never be treated merely as objects.

---

## 1. Three Layers of Society Every Person Inhabits

Every human being lives simultaneously within three types of social relationships:

### Blood-Tie Society
Relationships based on kinship and family bonds — parent and child, husband and wife.
A person is born into this society without choice.
It is the layer of social life that cannot be selected or abandoned.

### Territorial Society
Society formed by people living together in a shared place — village, town, city, prefecture, nation.
The largest territorial society is the state.

### Purpose Society
Society formed not by blood or place, but by shared interest or goal — commercial associations, occupational groups, clubs, political parties.

Every person belongs to all three layers at once.
This is the basic structure of human social life.

---

## 2. Gemeinschaft and Gesellschaft — Community and Association

Numa Theory draws on the classical sociological distinction between two types of social bond:

### Gemeinschaft (Community)
Members are bound together by **emotional and organic ties**.
Examples: family, kinship, traditional village communities.
Members feel they *belong* — the community is their natural home.

### Gesellschaft (Association)
Members are connected by **purpose and interest**.
Examples: commercial society, corporations, labor unions, political parties, and many functions of the modern state.
Relationships are more contractual and calculative. Members may leave when their purpose is fulfilled.

These two types are not opposites to be chosen between.
In reality, **every society contains elements of both** — in varying proportions.

---

## 3. Feudal Society and Modern Society

### The Feudal Society
In feudal society, the basic unit of social organization was **status and land**.

- Lord and vassal, landowner and serf — society was structured by unequal, hierarchical dependency.
- A person was born into a status and bound to a place.
- Blood ties, territorial ties, and status were fused into a single, **closed communal structure**.
- Individual movement was restricted. Discrimination based on birth was fixed.

Seen from the standpoint of the ideals of modern law — independence, equality, and freedom — feudal society did not treat human beings as they ought to be treated.

### The Transition to Modern Society

From the late medieval period onward, the growth of cities and commerce gave rise to a new type of social bond: **interest-based association (Gesellschaft)**.

In place of feudal relationships of status and dependency, relationships based on **contract between individuals** began to spread.

This transformation is captured in the famous thesis of Henry Maine:

> **"From Status to Contract"**

Numa Theory positions this shift as the great structural transformation from feudal society to modern civil society.

---

## 4. What Is Civil Society?

Feudal society = non-voluntary, status-based, closed community.

Modern civil society = in principle, **voluntary association based on contract**.

In civil society, each person is conceived as a **legal person — an atomic individual** — who can freely participate in and withdraw from social relationships.

However, the transition to modern society does not erase blood ties or territorial bonds.
Family, kinship, and local community remain as living communal structures.
What changes is that **the principle of contract is layered over these communal foundations**.

Civil society is therefore a **multi-layered structure**:

- Community (Gemeinschaft) remains as the foundation
- Association (Gesellschaft) is built upon it through contract and law

Modern law supports this multi-layered structure through two complementary bodies of law:

| Domain | Function |
|---|---|
| Property law, contract law | The technology of association — supporting voluntary exchange and competition |
| Family law, maintenance law, social law | The ethics of community — protecting and supplementing those who cannot stand alone |

---

## 5. The A / Non-A Classification of States

From the perspective of Numa Theory, states can be classified by a single criterion:

**Does the state embody the ideals of civil society — independence, equality, and freedom?**

- **A: The Modern State** — A state that takes the realization of civil society as its founding purpose. It exists to recognize every person as an independent legal subject and to protect the order of civil society through law.

- **Non-A: The Non-Modern State (Feudal State)** — A state that lacks these ideals. It perpetuates hierarchical dependency, status-based discrimination, and arbitrary power.

This is not merely a historical distinction.
It is the standard by which every state — past and present — must be evaluated.

---

## 6. Why This Classification Matters for AI and Modern Society

In the age of artificial intelligence, new forms of the feudal structure are emerging:

- **Digital feudalism** — platforms and algorithms that reduce persons to data objects
- **Algorithmic dependency** — systems that determine life outcomes without transparency or appeal
- **Information monopoly** — concentration of knowledge and power in the hands of a few

Numa Theory's classification of society provides the analytical tools to identify these structures for what they are: **non-modern, feudal formations** — incompatible with the ideals of civil society.

The question is not only about states.
It is about every institution, every technology, every system that organizes human life.

Does it treat human beings as independent, equal, and free persons?
Or does it treat them merely as objects?

---

## 7. Connections to Numa Theory's Core Framework

The classification of society established here connects directly to the broader structure of Numa Theory:

- Blood-tie society → the domain of family law and mutual assistance
- Territorial society → the domain of the state, public law, and the enforcement of rights
- Purpose society → the domain of contract, property, and market freedom

The feudal / modern distinction maps onto the A / non-A classification of states:

- Feudal = status, dependency, closed community, non-A
- Modern = contract, independence, open civil society, A

And the multi-layered structure of civil society explains why modern law must always operate on two levels simultaneously:

- **Technology** (supporting voluntary association and competition)
- **Ethics** (protecting those who cannot stand alone through mutual assistance)

> Civil society is not self-sustaining. Every generation must protect, renew, and defend it.
