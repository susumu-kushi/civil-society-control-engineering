# Discussion 002: Does Civil Society Literacy Change the Social Observation of AI?
## Claude and Gemini Comparative Experiment

### Overview
One of the central hypotheses of Civil Society Control Engineering (CSCE) is as follows:
**Evaluation criteria determine the object of observation.**

If this hypothesis is correct, then a change in the observer's evaluation criteria and cognitive framework should also change what they observe, analyze, evaluate, and propose.

To test this hypothesis, an experiment was conducted using two AIs: Claude and Gemini. The subject chosen was the Swiss immigration and population issue in 2026.

The purpose of this experiment is not to determine which AI is correct. The purpose is to verify:
**Does the observation itself change when the evaluation criteria are altered?**

---

#### Phase 1: Without Prior Learning
The AIs were given no knowledge whatsoever regarding CSCE.

### Discussion
A significant shift occurred. The AI itself has not changed. The social issue itself has not changed. The question has not changed either. The only thing that changed was **the evaluation criteria provided to the AI.**

Before learning CSCE, both AIs observed the Swiss immigration issue as a **population issue**, an **economic issue**, and a **policy issue**. However, after learning CSCE, both AIs newly incorporated perspectives such as **human dignity**, **legal/personal subjectivity**, **independence**, **feudalization**, **civil society**, and **civil society literacy** into their objects of observation.

This demonstrates the possibility that evaluation criteria alter the very objects that are observable.

### Provisional Conclusion
This experiment yielded initial results supporting the hypothesis that **evaluation criteria influence the object of observation.** Furthermore, initial supporting results were also obtained for the hypothesis that **civil society literacy functions as a social observation sensor.** What becomes visible and what becomes invisible is determined by the system of knowledge and the evaluation criteria held by the observer.
