# Numa Theory: The Origin

## The Fundamental Structure of Modern Law and Civil Society

-----

## Core Principle

> Human beings must never be treated merely as objects.

-----

## 1. The Highest Ideal of Modern Law

The highest ideal of modern law is this:

**Every person who constitutes modern society must be independent, equal, and free.**

To realize this ideal, modern law does not treat human beings as they naturally are.
Instead, it processes them legally — transforming concrete individuals into abstract legal persons.

This is the starting point of Numa Theory.

-----

## 2. “Human Being” and “Person” — A Critical Distinction

Numa Theory draws a fundamental distinction between two concepts:

### Human Being

A biological existence — shaped by sex, age, physical strength, intelligence, wealth, and social condition.
Full of natural differences. Far from the ideal of independence, equality, and freedom.

### Person (Legal Person)

An abstract figure constructed by law.
Natural attributes are temporarily set aside.
What remains is a standard model: an independent, equal, and free subject of rights and duties.

Modern law aims to raise every human being to the level of this “Person.”

This is not a denial of human individuality.
It is a legal technique to guarantee that every individual is treated with equal dignity.

-----

## 3. The Atomic Individual and Contract Freedom

The standard legal person — the “atomic individual” — is a subject who:

- Expresses their own will in their own words
- Enters into agreements with others on equal terms
- Exchanges goods and services without interference
- Pursues their own life plan under their own responsibility

This is what legal scholars call **the principle of freedom of contract**.

The atomic individual is permitted to engage in the competition of life on their own terms.

-----

## 4. Those Who Cannot Meet the Standard — Legal Protection

Modern law divides people into two categories:

1. **The standard person (atomic individual)**
1. **Those who cannot meet the standard**

For those who cannot meet the standard, law applies what Numa Theory calls **legal processing** — bringing them as close as possible to the atomic individual:

- Infants and persons without legal capacity → assigned a legal representative
- Those without property or external resources → supported through maintenance obligations and social welfare

This reflects a biological reality: alongside competition for survival, nature also produces the instinct of **mutual assistance**.

-----

## 5. Two Instincts at the Heart of Modern Law

Modern law introduces and manages two fundamental instincts drawn from living society:

|Instinct                |Legal Expression                                           |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|
|Competition for survival|Contract law, property law, market freedom                 |
|Mutual assistance       |Family law, labor law, social security, consumer protection|

By balancing these two instincts, modern law pursues its great ideal:

**The independence, equality, and freedom of every person.**

-----

## 6. Law as Technology and Ethics

Numa Theory characterizes modern law as operating on two levels simultaneously:

### Law as Technology

- Standardizes human beings as legal persons
- Establishes rules of contract, ownership, and responsibility
- Creates the institutional framework for social competition
- Primary domain: property law, contract law

### Law as Ethics

- Places limits on competition when it threatens human dignity
- Protects those who cannot protect themselves
- Reflects the instinct of mutual assistance
- Primary domain: family law, labor law, social security, consumer protection

Modern law is therefore a **dual structure**:

> **Technology of competition + Ethics of mutual assistance**

-----

## 7. The Triad: Person — Society — Law

Numa Theory begins with a fundamental observation about the relationship between persons, society, and law.

- No person exists apart from society.
- No law exists apart from society.
- No society can remain stable without law.

This relationship is expressed in two classical maxims:

> *Ubi societas ibi ius.* — Where there is society, there is law.
> *Ubi ius ibi societas.* — Where there is law, there is society.

Person, society, and law form an **interdependent triad**.

-----

## 8. The Proper Order: Independence → Equality → Freedom

Numa Theory insists on a specific sequence:

**Independence must come first.**
Without independence, individuals become dependent upon power, wealth, or status.

**Equality must come second.**
Without equality among independent persons, power becomes concentrated and domination emerges.

**Freedom becomes meaningful only when independence and equality are secured.**
Without equality, freedom is merely the privilege of the powerful.

Therefore, the correct order is:

> **Independence → Equality → Freedom**

This sequence is not arbitrary.
It reflects the historical struggle against feudal systems — and it remains the standard by which modern law must be evaluated.

-----

## 9. Civil Society and the State

Civil society is the world of independent, equal, and free atomic individuals living together.

The modern state emerged as the **antithesis of the feudal state**.

The feudal state treated human beings according to status, hereditary rank, and dependency.
The modern state exists to recognize human beings as independent persons and to protect the order of civil society through law.

Therefore:

- A state that embodies the ideals of civil society = **a modern state**
- A state that lacks those ideals = **a non-modern state (feudal state)**

This is an A / non-A classification.
The standard of judgment is always the ideal of civil society: independence, equality, and freedom.

-----

## 10. The Mission of Numa Theory

Numa Theory is a framework for understanding and defending civil society.

Its mission is threefold:

1. **Analyze reality as it is** (Sein) — examine the actual structures of power, society, and law
1. **Extract the standard model of the human person** from that reality
1. **Evaluate and reconstruct law** in light of the ideal (Sollen) — independence, equality, and freedom

Law is not merely a collection of rules.
Law is not merely a technique of interpretation.

Law is the ongoing project of realizing civil society — for every person, in every generation.

> Human beings must never be treated merely as objects.
