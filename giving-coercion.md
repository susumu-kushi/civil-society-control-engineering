# Giving Coercion

## Definition

Giving Coercion refers to the use of law and public power to protect, support, and supplement individuals who are unable to fully exercise their independence, equality, or freedom.

Its purpose is not domination.

Its purpose is the protection of human dignity.

---

## Core Principle

Public power may be used to protect human dignity and support independent participation in society.

---

## Numa Theory Perspective

Civil society is founded upon:

Human Dignity → Independence → Equality → Freedom

However, not all individuals possess the same ability to protect their own interests.

Children, elderly persons, persons with disabilities, and others may require assistance and protection.

Giving Coercion exists to supplement human vulnerability.

---

## Legal Perspective

Examples of Giving Coercion include:

* compulsory education
* social security
* public healthcare
* welfare systems
* labor protection laws
* consumer protection laws
* legal protection of minors

These measures restrict absolute freedom in order to protect human dignity and equal participation.

---

## Historical Perspective

Classical liberalism emphasized freedom from state interference.

Modern societies gradually recognized that freedom alone is insufficient when individuals lack the resources necessary for independence.

As a result, many legal systems developed institutions designed to protect vulnerable persons.

---

## AI Era Perspective

Artificial Intelligence may improve the ability to identify individuals who need support.

However, assistance must never become control.

The purpose of Giving Coercion is empowerment, not dependency.

AI should help strengthen independence, not replace it.
