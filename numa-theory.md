# Numa Theory

Numa Theory views law as a practical philosophy for realizing civil society.

Law is not merely a collection of rules, nor merely a technique of interpretation.

Its purpose is to analyze reality and evaluate society according to the principles of civil society.

## Historical Perspective

Human history has passed through many forms of social organization.

In feudal societies, human beings were often treated according to status, power, and dependency.

Modern society emerged as an antithesis to such systems.

The purpose of modern law is to ensure that human beings are not treated merely as objects of power, labor, profit, or control.

## Fundamental Order

Numa Theory emphasizes three principles:

### Independence

Human beings must be recognized as independent persons.

Without independence, individuals become dependent upon power, wealth, or status.

### Equality

Independent persons must be treated as equals before the law.

Without equality, power becomes concentrated and domination emerges.

### Freedom

Freedom becomes meaningful only when independence and equality are secured.

Without equality, freedom becomes the privilege of the powerful.

Therefore, the proper order is:

Independence → Equality → Freedom

## The Role of Law

Law exists to protect civil society.

Its role is not only to maintain order but also to prevent society from returning to structures of domination.

Law functions as a safeguard against both private and public concentrations of power.

## Core Principle

Human beings must never be treated merely as objects.

## Mission

Civil society is not self-sustaining.

Each generation must protect, renew, and develop it.

Numa Theory provides a framework for understanding and defending civil society in a changing world.
