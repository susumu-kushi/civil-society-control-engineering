# Who Defines the Goal?

## Introduction

Every control system requires a goal.

A thermostat requires a target temperature.

An autopilot requires a destination.

A medical system requires a definition of health.

Without a goal, control becomes arbitrary.

Without a goal, power becomes dangerous.

The most important question is therefore not:

> Who controls society?

The more important question is:

> Who defines the goal?

---

## The Problem of Power

Throughout history, many institutions have attempted to define social goals.

* Kings
* Governments
* Religions
* Markets
* Political parties
* Corporations

Each claimed to know what society ought to become.

Yet history repeatedly shows the same danger.

When power itself defines the goal, human beings become instruments of that goal.

People become tools.

Resources.

Objects.

Means to an end.

Civil Society Control Engineering rejects this approach.

---

## The Goal Must Stand Above Power

The goal cannot be determined by:

* The state
* The market
* Artificial intelligence
* Any single institution

All of these are objects of evaluation.

All of these are subjects of control.

The goal must stand above them.

In Numa Theory, this higher standard belongs to the world of Sollen.

It is the normative compass by which society is evaluated.

---

## Human Dignity as the Ultimate Goal

The foundation of the entire framework is simple:

> Human beings must never be treated merely as objects.

This principle is called Human Dignity.

Human Dignity is not created by the state.

It is not granted by the market.

It is not calculated by AI.

It exists prior to all institutions.

Human Dignity therefore becomes the highest reference point of social evaluation.

---

## The Four-Level Goal Structure

From Human Dignity emerges the entire structure of civil society.

Human Dignity

↓

Independence

↓

Equality

↓

Freedom

These are not parallel values.

They form a sequence.

Without Human Dignity, Independence collapses.

Without Independence, Equality becomes impossible.

Without Equality, Freedom becomes domination.

The goal of civil society is therefore not merely freedom.

The goal is the realization of all four levels together.

---

## Evaluating Society

Civil Society Control Engineering evaluates society by asking:

1. Are human beings treated as persons rather than objects?

2. Are all people recognized as independent legal persons?

3. Are independent persons treated equally?

4. Is freedom protected within that framework?

These questions provide the goal against which all institutions are measured.

---

## Who Defines the Goal?

The answer is neither the state nor the market.

The answer is not AI.

The goal is defined by the normative principles of civil society itself.

Human Dignity.

Independence.

Equality.

Freedom.

These principles serve as the North Star of the Civil Society Control Loop.

---

## Conclusion

Power must be controlled.

Controllers must be monitored.

But before either question can be answered, a more fundamental question must be resolved:

> Who defines the goal?

Numa Theory answers:

The goal is not defined by power.

The goal is defined by the principles of civil society.

Human Dignity.

Independence.

Equality.

Freedom.

These principles guide the entire control loop and provide the standard by which society is continuously evaluated and improved.
