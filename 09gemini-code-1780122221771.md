## 1. Where the Religious Mind Is Born

[cite_start]The human religious mind (宗教心) is born precisely when an individual confronts the severe dimensions of existence that completely transcend human power, intellect, or control[cite: 173]:
- [cite_start]Life and death (生と死) [cite: 173]

## 2. The Fundamental Distinctions Between Law and Religion

[cite_start]While both law and religion are powerful normative systems that direct human behavior, Numa Theory draws a sharp analytical distinction between them based on two criteria[cite: 175]:

### The Dimension of Operation (扱う次元の違い)

* [cite_start]**Religion:** Directly addresses the ultimate, transcendent domain[cite: 177, 178]. [cite_start]It deals with concepts of "salvation" (救い), "absolute good and evil" (絶対的善悪), and the "eternal" (永遠)—dimensions that exist far beyond the physical world[cite: 178].
* [cite_start]**Law:** Operates entirely within the empirical, terrestrial domain[cite: 179, 180]. [cite_start]Its sole concern is to adjust concrete social relationships and establish a stable framework to ensure that **coexistence in this world (この世の共同生活)** remains possible[cite: 180].

### The Objective of the Norm (目標の違い)

* [cite_start]**Religion:** Aims for a radical, inward transformation of the individual[cite: 182]. [cite_start]It demands an ultimate reorientation of the heart, spirit, and conscience, such as repentance or enlightenment[cite: 182].
* [cite_start]**Law:** Stays strictly at the perimeter of human interiority[cite: 183]. [cite_start]It does not demand internal spiritual purity; rather, it contents itself with establishing the **external boundaries of action (行為の外枠)**—defining what is legally permissible and what is prohibited[cite: 183].

| Dimension | Religion | Law |
|---|---|---|
| **Realm** | [cite_start]Transcendent / Eternal (超越・永遠) [cite: 178] | [cite_start]Empirical / Terrestrial (現世・共同生活) [cite: 180] |
| **Focus** | [cite_start]Inward Transformation / Soul (内面・魂) [cite: 182] | [cite_start]External Action / Social Boundaries (外部行為の枠) [cite: 183] |

## 3. The Intertwined History of Sacred and Secular Norms

[cite_start]Despite these deep systemic differences, Numa Theory emphasizes that law and religion are by no means entirely isolated from one another[cite: 184]. [cite_start]Historically and conceptually, they exist in a state of continuous interaction[cite: 185]:

### The Religious Foundations of Legal Substance
[cite_start]Throughout history, secular legal norms have been heavily shaped and fertilized by religious worldviews and theological ethics[cite: 185]. [cite_start]Substantive values born within religious traditions—such as neighborly love, mercy, forgiveness, and absolute honesty—have gradually evolved into cornerstone concepts of modern civil law[cite: 186]:
- [cite_start]The principle of good faith and fair dealing in contracts (信義誠実の原則) [cite: 186]
- [cite_start]The protection of vulnerable social groups and the impoverished (弱者保護) [cite: 186]
- [cite_start]The modern secular doctrine of universal, inalienable human rights (人権観念) [cite: 186]

### The Legal Boundaries Formed Around Religion
[cite_start]Modern law performs an essential structuring function around religion[cite: 187]. [cite_start]Through constitutional frameworks such as **religious freedom (信教の自由)** and the **separation of church and state (政教分離)**, the legal system guarantees an open space for spiritual practice while simultaneously drawing a firm boundary line[cite: 187]. [cite_start]It ensures that no religious entity can weaponize its dogmas to violate the fundamental human rights of others or dismantle the democratic order of civil society[cite: 187]. [cite_start]Religion acts as a deep wellspring of values for the human heart, but law provides the indispensable baseline that allows individuals of conflicting faiths to peacefully coexist[cite: 188].

## 4. The Secular Framework of Sollen

[cite_start]Custom, morality, law, and religion all belong to the world of *Sollen*—the normative realm of "what ought to be." [cite: 190] Within this landscape, Numa Theory carefully positions their respective layers:

* [cite_start]**Religion:** Represents the deepest layer of *Sollen*, directly touching upon human existential concerns and ultimate meaning[cite: 191].
* [cite_start]**Law:** Functions as a secular, public layer of *Sollen*, designed specifically to secure human coexistence within civil society[cite: 192].

[cite_start]The ultimate objective of modern law is not to judge, evaluate, or validate the theological truth of any religious doctrine[cite: 193]. [cite_start]Instead, its role is to secure a completely neutral playing field[cite: 194, 195]. [cite_start]By establishing that any person has the right to believe or not believe, the modern state creates the secular framework within which independent, equal, and free citizens can achieve genuine solidarity and shared life[cite: 195, 198].
