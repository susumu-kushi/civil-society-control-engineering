# CSCE Observation Theory: Feudal and Modern Evaluation Criteria

## 1. Foundational Proposition

The starting point of Civil Society Control Engineering (CSCE) is the following proposition:

> Human beings are not objects.

Human beings are not entities to be valued according to status, attributes, affiliations, or rank. They must be treated as independent persons possessing dignity and agency.

---

## 2. The First Principle of Observation

In CSCE Observation Theory, the first question to ask is:

> How does a society evaluate human beings?

Does it evaluate people through attributes and status?

Or does it evaluate them as autonomous persons?

This question serves as the primary observation point for diagnosing the health of a society.

---

## 3. Feudal Evaluation Criteria

Feudal evaluation refers to judging people primarily by their attributes, status, or affiliations rather than by their actions or ideas.

### Examples

- Which university did they attend?
- What title or position do they hold?
- Who was their mentor?
- Which organization do they belong to?
- What is their family background?
- How famous are they?

Under feudal evaluation, the question:

> "Who said it?"

is considered more important than:

> "What was said?"

---

## 4. Modern Evaluation Criteria

Modern evaluation refers to judging people as independent persons and evaluating their claims, actions, and responsibilities.

### Examples

- Is the argument logically coherent?
- Is there evidence?
- Is accountability fulfilled?
- Are agreements and obligations respected?
- Are others treated as persons rather than objects?

Under modern evaluation, the question:

> "What was said?"

is considered more important than:

> "Who said it?"

---

## 5. Civil Society Literacy

Civil Society Literacy is the ability to understand and evaluate human beings as persons rather than as collections of attributes, status, or affiliations.

The extent to which an individual or a society relies on feudal evaluation or modern evaluation is an important indicator of how deeply the principles of civil society have been internalized.

---

## 6. Position within CSCE

Feudal and Modern Evaluation Criteria constitute one of the fundamental indicators in CSCE Social Health Diagnostics.

When diagnosing a society, the first question should be:

> Does this society view human beings primarily as attributes and statuses?

Or:

> Does it view them as autonomous persons possessing dignity and agency?

This question forms the foundation of CSCE Observation Theory.

---

## Key Principle

> Feudal Evaluation judges the person through attributes.
>
> Modern Evaluation judges the person through personhood, actions, responsibility, and reason.

The transition from feudal society to modern civil society can therefore be understood not only as a change in institutions, but also as a transformation in the way human beings evaluate one another.
