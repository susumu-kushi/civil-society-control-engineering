# Modernity

## Definition

Modernity is the social and legal transformation that recognizes human beings as independent persons rather than members of a fixed status hierarchy.

Modernity emerged through the rejection of feudal domination and the recognition of legal equality, individual rights, and constitutional government.

---

## Core Principle

Human beings are recognized as persons rather than as subjects of status.

---

## Historical Perspective

For much of human history, societies were organized through status, hierarchy, privilege, and dependency.

The modern era emerged through a series of intellectual, political, and legal transformations.

These developments established:

* legal equality
* individual rights
* constitutional government
* freedom of thought
* freedom of economic activity

Modernity fundamentally changed the relationship between the individual and society.

---

## Numa Theory Perspective

Modernity represents a major advance over feudal society.

However, modernity is not the final goal.

Modern society still contains:

* inequality
* concentrations of power
* economic domination
* social exclusion

Therefore, modernity must continue moving toward civil society.

The direction is:

Feudalism → Modernity → Civil Society

---

## Legal Perspective

Modern law recognizes individuals as legal subjects.

It protects:

* human dignity
* independence
* equality
* freedom

These principles form the foundation of modern constitutional and civil law systems.

---

## AI Era Perspective

Artificial Intelligence is transforming modern society.

AI can strengthen freedom and opportunity.

It can also strengthen surveillance, concentration of power, and dependency.

The challenge is not whether technology advances.

The challenge is whether modern society continues moving toward civil society.
