# Modern Law Does Not Reject Capitalism

## — An Analysis of a Dialogue Between Atsuhiko Minowa and Kohei Saito Through the Lens of Civil Society Control Engineering —

### The Beginning of the Discussion

After watching a dialogue between Atsuhiko Minowa and Kohei Saito, I asked Gemini to analyze their discussion.

The AI examined the conversation from several perspectives:

* Digital Feudalism
* Human Agency
* AI and Democracy
* Local Communities
* Pluralistic Values

One of the most interesting observations was that both speakers, despite their differences, appeared to be struggling with the same fundamental question:

**How can human beings remain subjects rather than objects in an age increasingly shaped by technology, capital, and algorithms?**

This concern aligns closely with one of the core principles of Civil Society Control Engineering:

> Human beings must never be treated merely as objects.

---

### What the AI Observed

The AI interpreted Minowa's argument in favor of visionary figures such as Elon Musk as a tendency toward a form of digital feudalism.

According to the AI, concentrating social decision-making power in the hands of a small number of technological elites risks reducing ordinary citizens to passive objects within a system designed and controlled by others.

By contrast, Saito's emphasis on democratic participation and collective decision-making was interpreted as an attempt to preserve human agency and prevent excessive concentrations of power.

The AI also viewed Minowa's concept of three worlds—

* Capitalism (the market)
* Social recognition (SNS society)
* Local communities

—as an effort to introduce multiple feedback systems into society rather than allowing a single value system to dominate everything.

---

### The Missing Perspective

While the AI's analysis was insightful, I felt that both the discussion itself and the AI's interpretation were missing a crucial historical perspective.

Neither side sufficiently addressed the question:

**What was the historical purpose of modern society and modern law?**

Civil Society Control Engineering approaches social development through the following sequence:

**Feudalism → Modernity → Civil Society**

This sequence matters.

Without understanding why modern society emerged, it becomes difficult to understand what should be preserved, what should be improved, and what should be controlled.

---

### Modern Law Was Created to Overcome Feudalism

Modern law was not created to abolish markets.

Modern law was created to liberate human beings from feudal domination.

It established principles such as:

* Equality before the law
* Individual legal personality
* Private property rights
* Freedom of contract
* Limits on political power through law

These principles transformed individuals from subjects of rulers into independent legal persons.

In other words, modern law became the institutional foundation upon which modern society and market economies were built.

---

### Capitalism Is Not the Enemy

From this perspective, capitalism itself is not the fundamental problem.

Capitalism developed within the legal framework established by modern law.

Without:

* Property rights
* Contractual freedom
* Economic freedom
* Legal stability

modern market economies could not exist.

Therefore, modern law does not reject capitalism.

The real problem emerges when economic and technological power become so concentrated that human beings are once again treated as objects rather than subjects.

---

### Giving Coercion and Taking Coercion

Civil Society Control Engineering argues that modern law functions as a social control system designed to preserve human dignity.

It performs this role through two complementary mechanisms.

#### Giving Coercion

Examples include:

* Public education
* Social security
* Healthcare systems
* Welfare programs
* Disability support

These measures help individuals maintain the independence necessary to participate as subjects in society.

#### Taking Coercion

Examples include:

* Antitrust law
* Labor law
* Consumer protection law
* Progressive taxation
* AI regulation

These measures prevent excessive concentrations of power and protect the conditions necessary for civil society.

---

### The Goal Is Civil Society

Civil Society Control Engineering does not seek to abolish capitalism.

Nor does it advocate a return to feudal communities.

Its goal is:

**To move beyond feudalism, live within modernity, and realize civil society.**

Civil society is founded upon three principles:

**Independence → Equality → Freedom**

In that order.

Without independence, equality becomes fragile.

Without equality, freedom becomes privilege.

---

### Conclusion

The dialogue between Minowa and Saito raises important questions about the future of society.

However, the discussion largely bypasses the role of modern law as a control mechanism.

The central question is not:

**Should capitalism be abolished?**

The more important question is:

**How should modern law be updated so that AI systems, global capital, platform corporations, and other emerging centers of power remain under democratic and legal control?**

Modern law does not reject capitalism.

Modern law is a legal system designed to realize civil society while protecting human dignity.
