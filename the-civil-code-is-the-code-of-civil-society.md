# Core Thesis

> The Civil Code is the code of civil society.

As a graduate student, I repeatedly heard these words from my mentor, Professor Masaya Numa.

At the time, I did not understand what they meant.

To me, civil law was simply a collection of rules governing contracts, property, family relations, and inheritance.

Why, then, did Professor Numa call it *the code of civil society*?

It took me decades to begin to understand.

After a career in business management, information technology, and artificial intelligence, I returned to the study of civil law and discovered that the Civil Code is far more than a legal textbook.

It is a blueprint for human society itself.

This book is an attempt to explain that realization.

---

# What Does the Civil Code Describe?

The Civil Code is not primarily a code about the state.

Rather, it describes the structure of civil society.

It begins with the assumption that human beings are independent persons capable of making decisions, entering relationships, and taking responsibility for their actions.

The Civil Code can be understood through three major components.

---

## Property Law

Property law governs:

* Ownership
* Contracts
* Obligations
* Liability for wrongful acts

It provides the legal framework for free economic activity among independent persons.

People buy, sell, exchange, cooperate, and create value through voluntary relationships.

The state is not the central actor.

The central actor is the individual person.

Property law assumes a society in which people act according to their own judgment and responsibility.

---

## Family Law

Human society cannot be sustained by economic activity alone.

Children cannot enter contracts.

The sick, elderly, and vulnerable cannot always live independently.

Family law addresses these realities through:

* Marriage
* Parent-child relationships
* Support obligations

Family law institutionalizes mutual aid and social responsibility.

It recognizes that human beings are not merely economic actors but members of families and communities.

---

## Inheritance Law

Every legal person eventually dies.

Persons themselves are not inherited.

Property, however, must continue to exist within society.

Inheritance law determines how rights and property are transferred after death.

It provides continuity between generations and contributes to social stability.

---

# The Principle of Private Autonomy

Property law, family law, and inheritance law are all built upon a common foundation:

**the principle of private autonomy.**

Human beings are not expected to live by governmental command.

They think for themselves.

They make their own choices.

They assume responsibility for their own actions.

The Civil Code therefore presupposes a society of independent persons interacting on equal footing.

This is the foundation of modern civil society.

---

# Conflict Is Inevitable

No society is free from disputes.

Conflicts arise from:

* Breach of contract
* Violations of rights
* Family disputes
* Inheritance conflicts

The crucial question is not whether conflict exists.

The crucial question is how conflict is resolved.

In feudal societies, disputes were often settled through power, status, or force.

The stronger party prevailed.

Such a system places human beings under domination by other human beings.

Modern society rejects this principle.

---

# Why the Modern State Exists

The modern state emerges for a specific purpose.

Its mission is not to dominate civil society.

Its mission is to protect it.

The state:

* Prohibits private retaliation
* Provides courts and legal procedures
* Resolves disputes under the rule of law

The state is not the master of society.

It is an institution created to preserve civil society.

Civil society comes first.

The state exists to protect it.

---

# Why Osugi Sakae and Kropotkin Matter

Professor Numa encouraged me to read Osugi Sakae and Peter Kropotkin.

At the time, I could not understand why.

Years later, their relevance became clear.

Osugi Sakae challenged the assumption that human beings must always live under systems of domination.

Kropotkin demonstrated the importance of mutual aid in human society.

Together, they raise questions that lie beneath the Civil Code itself:

* What is civil society?
* What is human freedom?
* What is mutual aid?
* Why does society exist?

These are not merely legal questions.

They are questions about the foundations of human coexistence.

---

# The Challenge of the AI Age

The rise of artificial intelligence presents a new challenge.

Human beings risk being reduced to:

* Data
* Resources
* Objects of management
* Targets of control

The danger is not technological progress itself.

The danger is forgetting the principle upon which modern society was built:

> Human beings are not things.

The central task of our age is to preserve human dignity while embracing technological change.
